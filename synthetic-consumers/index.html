Definitions

What Are Synthetic Consumers?

Synthetic consumers are simulated shoppers generated by large language models, used to produce and pressure-test hypotheses about buying behavior before — not instead of — real consumer research. They respond to product pages, offers or messaging the way a modeled population might, so a team can explore many "what if" variations quickly, then validate the strongest ones with real people.

Synthetic consumers vs. personas vs. synthetic respondents vs. digital twins

These four terms get used interchangeably, but they describe different things:

TermWhat it isHow it's builtBest use
Marketing personaA static, human-authored archetype ("budget-conscious parent, 35–44")Written by a researcher or marketer from qualitative inputTeam alignment, messaging direction
Synthetic respondentAn LLM prompted to answer a survey instrument as if it were a member of a target populationPrompted or fine-tuned language model, often at scaleEarly-stage survey piloting, question design
Synthetic consumer (our usage)A simulated shopper that interacts with an actual product page, offer or decision scenario, not just a survey questionBehaviorally-grounded simulation layered on an LLM populationDiagnosing friction in a specific page or flow before a real A/B test
Digital twinA model of one specific, identifiable real customer or account, updated from that person's real dataBuilt from an individual's real behavioral historyPersonalization and account-level forecasting for an existing customer

When synthetic consumer simulation applies

  • Triaging many candidate page or message changes down to a short list worth real-world testing
  • Diagnosing why a page might be losing shoppers, not just that it is
  • Exploring edge cases or low-traffic scenarios where a real A/B test would take too long to reach significance

When it doesn't

  • As the sole basis for a final launch decision
  • For populations or cultural contexts the underlying language model is known to represent poorly
  • As a substitute for disclosure — simulated results should never be reported as measured real-world outcomes

How Marketing Simulation Lab uses the term

We use "synthetic consumer" to mean a simulated shopper evaluated against an actual commerce artifact — a product page, a landing page, an offer — rather than an abstract survey question. FAQ

Are synthetic consumers the same as marketing personas?
No. Personas are static, human-written archetypes used for alignment and messaging. Synthetic consumers are generated, model-driven responses that can be queried interactively and varied systematically across many simulated decision scenarios.
Can synthetic consumers replace real user research?
No. Synthetic consumers are a hypothesis-generation and triage tool. Findings should be validated with real users or real-world experiments before being treated as fact — simulation proposes, reality decides.
When should I not use synthetic consumers?
Avoid relying on synthetic consumers alone for final go/no-go decisions, for small or culturally specific populations the underlying model may not represent well, or for any claim you plan to publish as a measured result rather than a simulated hypothesis.

How Consumer Behavior Simulation Works

Consumer behavior simulation is a three-step pipeline — discover, simulate, validate — that uses a modeled population of synthetic consumers to test hypotheses about buying friction before committing real traffic to a real A/B test. It's built to triage ideas quickly, not to replace the real test that ultimately confirms them.

The pipeline

  1. Discover

    Connect the commerce artifact being evaluated — a product page, landing page or offer — with available customer-voice signals: reviews, support questions, known objections. Surface evidence-linked hypotheses about what's holding shoppers back.

  2. Simulate

    Change one decision variable at a time (a headline, a missing spec, a returns policy callout) and run a simulated consumer population against both versions. Compare how the population interprets information and where it hesitates or leaves.

  3. Validate

    Take the highest-signal changes into a controlled real-world experiment. Compare the simulated ranking of changes against the real, observed outcome, and use the gap to improve calibration for next time.

What simulation is good at — and isn't

Simulation is good at generating and ranking many candidate hypotheses quickly, and at surfacing friction a team hadn't considered. It is not good at producing a number you should publish as a measured conversion lift, and it should not be the only evidence behind a final launch decision. See the research page for the specific internal and external evidence behind that distinction, including where simulated and real effect sizes have diverged.

FAQ

What inputs does consumer behavior simulation need?
At minimum, the commerce artifact being evaluated (a product page, landing page or offer) and the decision variable being changed. Reviews, support questions or other real customer-voice signals improve how well the simulation is grounded, when available.
How long does one simulation cycle take compared to a real A/B test?
A simulation cycle is typically much faster than a real A/B test because it does not wait for live traffic to accumulate statistical significance. The tradeoff is that only a real test confirms the result against actual customer behavior.
Does simulation replace the real A/B test?
No. Simulation is used to prioritize which changes are most worth testing for real. The real A/B test — or another controlled real-world experiment — remains the step that validates the result.
